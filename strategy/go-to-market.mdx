# Go-to-market system

AIO3 should earn trust by **demonstrating teaching quality**, not by making big promises.

## Funnel hypothesis

1. **Awareness:** practical explainers, project demonstrations, educator collaborations, and parent conversations.
2. **Interest:** accessible program brief, sample learning outcomes, prerequisites and illustrative projects.
3. **Discovery:** a conversation about learner goals, readiness, equipment and supervision.
4. **Trial:** a low-risk, age-appropriate introductory activity.
5. **Enrollment:** documented curriculum scope, schedule, fees, consent, safeguarding and refund terms.
6. **Learning:** lessons, mentor reviews, safe projects and progress summaries.
7. **Continued engagement:** portfolio reflection, optional next pathways, learner feedback and alumni projects.

## Content pillars

- How computing systems actually work
- Building responsible AI workflows
- Debugging and engineering habits
- Sensors, robotics and physical computing
- Learner-created project showcases, only with appropriate permissions
- Honest explanations of what a course can and cannot teach

## Outreach guidelines

Do not use children’s personal data for marketing without legally appropriate permissions. Keep prospective learner records minimal, restrict access, disclose contact consent and provide opt-out routes.

## Experiments

Compare content formats, trial attendance, parent questions, educational value, and enrollment intent. Define hypotheses and metrics first. Do not treat interest survey answers as paid demand.

## Retention

Track learner experience, mentor response times, understanding, and project completion rather than relying only on repeat purchases.
