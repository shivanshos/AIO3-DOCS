# Business model hypothesis

AIO3 is investigating a **premium, mentorship-led, project-based** educational offering, initially oriented toward the Indian market. Nothing here asserts product-market fit or finalized pricing.

## Value proposition

Learners receive foundational technical understanding and guided hands-on practice. Parents receive clear visibility into goals, supervision expectations, progress, and learning evidence. Adult learners receive explicit prerequisites and project-focused progression.

## Potential offering layers

| Layer | Draft format | Value driver | Validation required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery workshops | Introductory sessions | Try-before-enrollment and readiness | Demand, conversion, engagement |
| Foundation pathways | Small in-person or online cohorts | Structured skills | Completion and learning gains |
| Premium mentorship | Limited one-to-one support | Personalized feedback | Mentor capacity and willingness to pay |
| Project labs | Equipment-supported experiences | Build and experiment | Safety, maintenance and margins |
| Institutional partnerships | Customized workshops | Broader access | Procurement and delivery economics |
| Digital knowledge resources | Public/free plus optional paid resources | Reach and continued practice | Quality and support cost |

## Unit economics to measure

`Contribution per cohort = collected fees - variable teaching cost - materials - lab/equipment allocation - payment fees - learner support cost`

Also model customer acquisition cost, conversion by segment, retention, mentor utilization, refund rate, venue cost, equipment replacement, and cash collection. Avoid presenting gross revenue as profit.

## Pricing discipline

Research local willingness-to-pay before quoting fees. Test packages with different mentorship intensities, clear inclusions and cancellation terms. Document scholarship/access options and ensure that premium positioning does not depend on deceptive scarcity.

## Go/no-go criteria

Proceed from pilots only when outcomes, parental trust, instructor workload, safety, and economics meet predefined thresholds. Establish those thresholds before the pilot, rather than judging retrospectively.
