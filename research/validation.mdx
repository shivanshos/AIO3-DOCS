# Evidence-driven discovery

AIO3 should proceed from hypotheses to decisions through explicit evidence. Founding enthusiasm is not a substitute for proof of demand or effective learning.

## Evidence maturity scale

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| V0 | Assumption or idea |
| V1 | Desk research or expert perspective |
| V2 | Direct target-audience interview or observation |
| V3 | Structured prototype or learning trial |
| V4 | Paid/operational pilot with defined measures |
| V5 | Repeated results across cohorts or contexts |

This is an internal proposed classification, not an externally validated standard.

## Priority research questions

- Which student/parent segments perceive a real gap in hands-on technical education?
- How do prerequisites differ between children, teens and adults?
- What outcomes matter most to learners, guardians and institutions?
- What alternatives exist locally and online, at what price and quality?
- Which mentorship structures deliver useful feedback sustainably?
- What equipment and safety conditions are feasible?
- What price points attract actual paid commitments?
- What learning gains are measurable within short pilots?
- What regulatory, child-protection, tax and business requirements apply?
- Can quality remain consistent as more mentors join?

## Research methods

Combine competitor mapping, document reviews, learner/parent interviews, prototype sessions, classroom observations and small paid pilots. Record sample bias, respondent context, consent, limitations and conflicting evidence.

## Pilot template

Specify target segment, hypothesis, eligibility, facilitator, duration, learning goals, safety review, consent, success criteria, cost, attendance and assessment methods before recruiting.

## Decision record

Every major decision should capture date, owner, evidence level, alternatives, decision, unresolved risks and review trigger. Avoid retroactively changing a hypothesis into a fact.
